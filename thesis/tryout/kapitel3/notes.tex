PCB-Prototype design:
    - Think about matching circuits
    - Follow ST reference design (MB1842 and MB1849)
    - For coverage testing: use U.FL (IPEX) connectors for easy antenna swapping
    - Use TCXO since LoRa is sensitive to freq Shift
    - GPS/GNSS: use u-blox MAX-M10S or Quectel L76-LB due to low power (with MOSFET control)
        - MAX10S for example requires VDD to be stable before Uart goes high 
        - Quectel L76KB very cheap and accessible (5€), but without antenna 
        - Or choose L86-M33 with integrated antenna to reduce risks
    - Use energy harvesting IC rather than LDO or normal buck conv
    - SPV1050 from ST: for solar charging with MPPT (max power point tracking)
    - BQ25570 from TI: for solar charging with MPPT, but not available in Germany + more expensive than SPV1050 on JLCPCB
    - For battery: use 18650 Li-ion or LiFePO4 for better safety, but they are bigger than coin cells
    - TPS22958 from TI as load switch to disconnect battery from subcircuits
    - external watchdog to prevent LoRa stack form hanging
    - SPI flash chip to log data locally in case of network failure
    - Keep GPS traces away from 868MHz RF traces
    
    - Add a small LDO (e.g. LP5907, 1 µA quiescent) between main rail and RF VDD. 
      This reduces ripple on the RF supply which measurably improves receiver sensitivity
    - Use a battery fuel gauge IC (e.g. MAX17048 - not critical comp on JLCPCB) 
      to monitor battery state of charge without needing a shunt resistor,
      SoC can also be included in payload for long deployments
    - Rubber duck (helical) antenna very popular for gateways 
    - maybe tag connect as programming interface?

BGA routing tips:
    - https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=0KbINAq0hcU Phil's Lab 
    - first row can be routed out normally
    - second row also, but with thinner traces
    - "dogbone" fanout:
        - route a short trace from the pad to a via, then route the rest of the way on the inner layer
        - this allows for more space between traces and easier routing
    - for very dense BGAs, consider using a "via in pad" approach
    - divide BGA into quadrants and route one quadrant at a time to reduce complexity
    - for example: top left quadrant -> traces go up and left to vias 
    - pwr and gnd vias close to each other + decoupling caps
    - place ALL vias first before routing
    - vias can be used as testpoints (although not recommended by IPC but sometimes you have no choice)
    - vias have to be tented on the top and bottom closed when used as test pads
